# Team Agreement

**COMP602 Software Development Practice — 2026 Semester 2**
**Project: CyberHeist**

## Team Goal

- To develop a working game, utilising the Godot engine in the Rust language.
- We are aiming for an A+ grade in this class.
- To get experience using the Agile methodology on a project — specifically Scrum.
- To learn new technologies that will benefit us in the future.
- To develop and improve our collaborative practices.

## Team Roles and Duties

**Product Owner — Adam Boyce**

- Owns the Product Backlog and the order it is prioritised in.
- Is the bridge to the client, and brings the prioritised backlog to Sprint Planning.
- Clarifies the scope of a user story when a developer is unsure.

**Scrum Master — Neel Kolhe**

- Organises meetings and informs the team when they are on.
- Hosts the stand-ups and the retrospective, and keeps each activity inside its time box.
- Tracks impediments, and escalates them when the team cannot clear them.
- Is the first point of contact when a conflict is raised.

**Developers — Adam Boyce, Neel Kolhe, Hunter Yates, Gerard Tibbles**

- Every member develops and tests user stories, in addition to any other role they hold.
- Each member takes at least one user story per sprint.
- Design, development, integration and testing are the whole team's work, not separate jobs.

**Minute taker** — rotates between all members, a different person each meeting.

## Member Rights

Every member of this team has the right:

- To be heard in planning and estimation, and to an equal vote in Planning Poker — we take a consensus, never an average, and no member overrides another's estimate.
- To a fair share of the development work, and to have their contribution visible on the Trello card and in the repository history.
- To say that a user story is too large or unclear, and to have it split or clarified rather than be expected to absorb it.
- To raise a concern, an impediment or a conflict without it being held against them.
- To reasonable notice of meetings, and to not be expected to be available outside our agreed working hours.
- To have their work reviewed within the agreed turnaround, so that nobody is left blocked.
- To have this agreement applied equally to them, whatever role they hold.

## Rules of Conduct, Expectations and Procedures

**Conduct**

- Be respectful of each other, and include each other in discussions as much as possible.
- Bring any potential issues that may arise to the Product Owner directly.
- If you are not sure of the scope of what you are working on, get the Product Owner to clarify it for you.

**Communication**

- Discord is our primary channel. Anything that affects the team goes there, not into a private chat.
- Respond to a message that needs an answer within 24 hours on weekdays.
- Decisions that change the backlog or the board get recorded on Trello, not only in chat.

**Meetings and attendance**

- Two stand-ups a week: one in our weekly lab session, and one online on Discord midweek.
- Minutes are taken every meeting by the rostered member and posted to the Documentation column on Trello.
- Tell the Scrum Master before any planned meeting you are not going to make.
- Email Matthew Kuo when you are going to be away from a lab.

**Effort**

- Each member commits to a minimum of 8 hours on the project per week.
- Sprints are sized on the course's planning assumption of 12 hours per member per week, allowing for other assessments and lectures.

**Development practices**

- Use Conventional Commits.
- Always pull before working.
- Follow the Rust Style Guide — <https://doc.rust-lang.org/style-guide/>.
- Never rewrite shared history: no force-pushing, and no amending a commit someone else may have pulled.
- A user story is only moved to Done when it meets our definition of done.

## Conflict Resolution Process

1. Talk to the person directly and privately first, in good faith.
2. If that does not resolve it, raise it with the Scrum Master. Anything about the project itself — scope, priority, what we are building — goes to the Product Owner instead.
3. The Scrum Master brings the team together, and we discuss it and agree a resolution by mutual agreement.
4. If the conflict escalates, a formal warning is given and documented in writing.
5. If it is still unresolved after that, the Scrum Master escalates it to our TA or the lecturer.

## Agreed Consequences for a Breach of Conduct

- 5 push-ups in the next lab.
- The count doubles for a repeat breach of the same rule, capped at 20.
- Repeated or serious breaches: a documented warning, and then reported to the TA or lecturer.

## Status of This Agreement

- This agreement is created and changed only by the mutual agreement of all team members.
- It is enforced by mutual agreement.
- It is made between equals, and applies to every member equally — including those holding the Product Owner and Scrum Master roles.
- It exists for the life of this team.
